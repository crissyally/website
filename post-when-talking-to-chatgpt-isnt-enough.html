When Talking to ChatGPT Isn't Enough: Finding a Trauma Therapist Who Fits

By Cristina Ally, LMHC · Flourish Counseling Co.

If you've been talking to ChatGPT about something that happened to you, you're far from alone. Late at night, when it feels like too much to say to anyone else, an AI chat can feel like a safe first place to put it into words.

That instinct is a good one. And if you've noticed that understanding it better hasn't made it hurt less, that's worth listening to as well.

You're not doing it wrong

Writing things out is real work. It can help you name feelings you couldn't name before, notice patterns in how you react, and prepare for conversations that feel hard. Many people arrive at therapy already knowing a great deal about their own story. That isn't a problem to fix. It's a head start.

Where a chat runs out

  • It can't notice what happens in your body. Trauma often lives in the tight chest, the shallow breath, the jolt at a sound. A trained therapist notices those moments and helps you through them as they happen.
  • It can't hold safety while memories are processed. Trauma-focused approaches like EMDR are designed to be paced by a trained clinician who keeps you grounded the whole way.
  • It tends to agree with you. That can be comforting, but growth sometimes needs gentle challenge from someone who knows you.
  • It isn't private in the way therapy is. What you type into most AI tools isn't covered by the confidentiality protections that apply to licensed therapists.
  • It isn't built for a crisis. If you're in danger or having thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988.

What to look for in a trauma therapist

  • Licensed in your state. In Florida, that includes Licensed Mental Health Counselors (LMHC), clinical social workers, marriage and family therapists, and psychologists.
  • Trained in trauma-focused approaches, such as EMDR, Accelerated Resolution Therapy (ART), or Cognitive Processing Therapy.
  • A short consultation first, so you can ask questions before you commit.
  • A space that feels safe and unhurried. You should never feel pushed to share more than you're ready to.
  • Clear fees, whether that's insurance, self-pay, or a superbill for out-of-network reimbursement.
  • A format that fits your life, in person or by secure telehealth.

How to bring your AI journal into therapy

You don't have to stop journaling with AI when you start therapy. It can become a useful bridge between sessions. One simple way:

  • Keep writing as you normally would during the week.
  • At the end of the week, ask for a short summary. For example: "Summarize what I wrote this week in a few bullet points: the moments that felt hardest, any patterns you notice, and questions I might want to bring to therapy."
  • Bring the summary, not the whole transcript, to your next session, and decide together what to explore.
  • Leave out names and identifying details about other people when you write.
