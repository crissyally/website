Services/Recovery Groups

Group & Recovery Groups

Some of this work feels lighter with other people in the room.

Therapist-led recovery groups for adult women in Winter Park and the greater Orlando area. Groups form periodically, and you can register your interest at any time.

Register your interest

Are you carrying something you've never said out loud? Do you wish you had a non-judgmental ear from someone who actually knows what this is like, because they've been there too? Do you wish you felt less alone in it? If so, a small group of women walking the same road might be a gentle place to start.

Who the groups are for

The groups I offer are closed recovery groups for adult women, including women recovering from sexual abuse.

A group might be right for you if you keep circling the same problem alone, or you wish you could trust someone with what you're carrying. Maybe you want to stop feeling like the only one, and you'd like, for once, to feel understood. Maybe you're interested in therapy and want something that feels lower-pressure than sitting one to one. Or maybe you're looking for skilled help, and also for people.

How a group works

In group therapy, sessions happen together with a few other people and a therapist guiding the work. Members share their experience, support each other, and are supported by people who understand it from the inside. Trauma and loss tend to leave people feeling isolated, and a group can be a gentle answer to that.

Groups at Flourish are led by a licensed therapist, and they're structured work with a clear focus. Closed means the group starts and finishes together with the same members throughout, which makes it possible to say hard things in a later week that nobody could have said in the first one.

What makes a group work

Group work helps you find connection with other people and their experience. In guided sessions you learn more about yourself and what helps you feel better, while being supported by women who understand what you're going through because they're going through it too.

Something else can happen that's harder to put into words. Watching another woman say the thing you've never said out loud, and watching the room receive it with care, can be healing in its own way.

Recovery after sexual abuse has its own shape, and much of it is about what came afterward: what you concluded about yourself, what you learned to tolerate, how close you let people get, what a boundary is, and what safety is supposed to feel like. Doing that alongside other women who already understand the parts you'd otherwise have to explain can take some of the weight off.

Who a group isn't for, at least not yet

A group isn't the right starting point for everyone. If you're in crisis, if things still feel too raw to share in a room with other people, or if what you need right now is focused one-on-one trauma work, individual therapy comes first, and a group may come later.

Group work and individual sessions can go side by side and support each other. The best way to find out what's right for you is to talk it through, which is why joining starts with a conversation.

How to register your interest

How groups work at Flourish. Groups form from time to time, and once a closed group has started, it can't add new members. There may or may not be one forming right now. Joining the interest list is how you hear about the next one first, and it doesn't commit you to anything.

You can request an appointment through the client portal and say that you're interested in a group. That puts you on the interest list for the next one, and it starts a conversation about whether group work fits what you're carrying right now. If it doesn't, I'll tell you honestly and we'll talk about what might.

Questions

Common questions about recovery groups

Who are the recovery groups for?

Adult women who want to heal alongside others who understand. The groups I lead are small and closed, so the same faces gather each week and trust has time to grow.

Is a group running right now?

Groups form from time to time, so there may or may not be one forming right now. Once a closed group has started, it can't add new members. Joining the interest list is how you hear about the next one first, and it doesn't commit you to anything.

How do I join a group?

Let me know you're interested through the client portal, and I'll reach out when the next group is forming. We'll also have a short conversation first about whether a group fits where you are right now.

What does a group cost?

I share the group fee when each group opens. Individual sessions are $185 for 50 minutes, self-pay, and I can provide a superbill on request.
