Services/Recovery Groups

Group & Recovery Groups

Some of this work goes faster with other people in the room.

Therapist-led recovery groups for adult women in Winter Park and the greater Orlando area. Groups form periodically, and you can register your interest at any time.

At a glance

  • Who it's for: Women looking for support and connection in a small, closed group
  • Where: Winter Park, near Orlando
  • With: Cristina Ally, LMHC, NCC, who has led recovery groups for women across the Orlando area
  • Fees: $185 per 50-minute session, self-pay for individual sessions; group fees are shared when a group opens. Superbills are available for out-of-network reimbursement, and HSA and FSA cards are welcome.
  • First step: a short phone consultation, about 10 minutes, to see if it feels like a good fit. You can request one through the client portal.

How groups run at Flourish. Groups form periodically rather than running continuously, and a closed group cannot add members once it has begun. There may or may not be one forming right now. The interest list is how you hear about the next one first, and registering your interest commits you to nothing.

Why a group

Are you carrying something you have never said out loud? Do you wish you had a non-judgmental ear that belonged to someone who actually knows what this is like, not because they read about it but because they have been there? Do you wish you felt less alone in it?

Group therapy differs from individual therapy in the obvious way and in a less obvious one. The obvious: sessions happen in a group led by a therapist rather than one to one. The less obvious: what makes it work is not only what the therapist brings. Members share their experience, support each other, and are supported by people who understand it from the inside. Isolation is one of the things trauma and loss reliably produce, and a group is a direct answer to it.

A group may be right for you if

  • You keep circling the same problem alone
  • You wish you could trust someone with what you are carrying
  • You want to stop feeling like the only one
  • You are interested in therapy and want something lower-pressure than sitting one to one
  • You wish you felt understood
  • You are looking for skilled help, and also for people

Groups at Flourish are led by a licensed therapist. They are structured work rather than open-ended conversation.

What makes a group work

The effectiveness of group work lies in its ability to help you find connection with other people and with their experience. In therapist-guided sessions you learn more about yourself and about how to improve the way you feel, while being supported by people who understand what you are going through because they are going through it too.

There is a second thing that happens, and it is harder to put into words. Watching another person say the thing you have never said out loud, and watching the room not flinch, does something that no amount of being told it was not your fault will ever do.

Recovery groups for women

Flourish runs closed recovery groups for adult women, including women recovering from sexual abuse. Closed means the group starts and finishes together with the same members throughout, which is what makes it possible to say hard things in a later week that nobody could have said in the first one.

Cristina Ally, LMHC has led recovery groups for women across the Orlando area for years, and women's trauma recovery is the center of her practice. Her work has included serving survivors of human trafficking.

Recovery work after sexual abuse is specific, and it is not the same as recounting what happened. Much of it is about what came afterward: what you concluded about yourself, what you learned to tolerate, how close you let people get, what a boundary is, and what safety is supposed to feel like. Doing that alongside other women who already understand the parts you would otherwise have to explain takes a weight off it that is hard to appreciate until it is gone.

Who a group is not for, at least not yet

A group is not a lower-cost substitute for individual therapy, and it is not the right starting point for everyone. If you are in crisis, if the material is still too raw to be in a room with other people, or if what you need right now is a protocol rather than a process, individual work comes first and a group may come later.

Group members frequently work individually at the same time, and the two support each other. The only way to find out which is right for you is to ask, which is why joining starts with a conversation rather than a sign-up form.

How to register your interest

You can request an appointment through the client portal and say that you are interested in a group. That gets you onto the interest list for the next one, and it opens a conversation about whether group work fits what you are carrying right now. If it does not, we will say so and talk about what does.

All work at Flourish is self-pay, and the fee for a group is confirmed at the point the group forms. See rates & insurance for how the practice handles payment and superbills.
