Some memories do not settle. Years pass, life moves on, and a sound or a smell or an offhand comment still drops you straight back into a moment you would rather not visit. EMDR exists for exactly that.

What EMDR is

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing is an integrative psychotherapy approach that has been extensively researched and is widely used in the treatment of trauma. It is not a conversation technique and it is not a school of thought. It is a set of standardized protocols that brings elements of several different treatment approaches together into one structured sequence.

Because it is standardized, you can know in advance roughly what a session will involve. For many people, that predictability is the reason they are willing to try it at all.

How EMDR works

EMDR uses a technique called bilateral stimulation, which repeatedly activates opposite sides of the brain. Therapists often do this with guided eye movements.

Those eye movements resemble the phase of sleep known as rapid eye movement, or REM, sleep. REM sleep is frequently understood to be the period when the mind processes the events of the day. EMDR appears to help the brain reprocess trapped memories in a similar way, so that normal information processing can resume and the memory can finally be filed where it belongs.

In practice, that means the memory does not disappear. It stops being live. You can recall the event without the event recalling you.

EMDR is also used to uncover and work with beliefs that formed as a result of relational trauma, or of abuse and neglect in childhood: the quiet conclusions people draw about themselves at an age when they have no way to argue back. For a fuller technical explanation of the approach, see the EMDR Institute.

What EMDR is used for

EMDR was originally established as a treatment for PTSD. It has since been used in work with:

  • Panic attacks
  • Complicated grief
  • Dissociative disorders
  • Disturbing memories
  • Phobias
  • Pain disorders
  • Performance anxiety
  • Addictions
  • Stress reduction
  • The effects of sexual or physical abuse
  • Body dysmorphic disorders
  • Personality disorders

What if none of that describes you?

Plenty of people who benefit from EMDR would not have picked a single item off that list. So try these instead.

Do you experience emotions that seem out of proportion to the situation in front of you, whether that is obvious to you, to the people around you, or to both?

Do you find yourself highly reactive to certain triggers, in a way you cannot talk yourself out of?

Is there a belief you hold about yourself that, intellectually, you know is not true, and that you believe anyway?

If you recognized yourself in any of those, you may still be a good candidate for EMDR.

Coming to EMDR after other therapy

Many people arrive at EMDR having already done a great deal of talking. They know their history. They can explain, clearly and calmly, exactly why they react the way they do. And they still react that way.

That gap between what you know and what you feel is one of the most reliable signs that a memory has not finished being processed. Insight is not the same as resolution, and no amount of explaining a thing to yourself will file it away. This is the specific problem EMDR is built to work on, which is why it is often useful for people whose previous therapy helped in every way except the one they wanted most.

It is also why EMDR works with beliefs and not only with events. Underneath a single intrusive memory there is usually a conclusion: that you are not safe, that it was somehow your fault, that you are too much or not enough. The event may be twenty years old. The conclusion is still running.

How long does EMDR take?

There is no honest general answer to that, and anyone who offers you one is guessing. The length of the work depends on what brought you here, on how much of it there is, on your goals, and on your own commitment to the process. Some people come in with one contained incident. Others are carrying something that started before they had words for it.

What can be said is that the pace is set with you rather than for you, and that you will know what the plan is at every stage.

What working with us looks like

Cristina Ally, LMHC is trained in EMDR, and uses it alongside Accelerated Resolution Therapy and the Reconsolidation of Traumatic Memories to resolve traumatic memories with adult clients.

EMDR does not begin on day one. The first phase of the work is safety and stabilization: building trust, and making sure you have grounding skills in place before any memory work starts. Protocol sessions are scheduled as extended sessions, because the protocol needs room to complete rather than being cut off by a clock. You set the pace throughout.

EMDR is one part of a broader trauma recovery approach at Flourish rather than a standalone product. Resolving a memory is the beginning of the work, not the end of it.

You can recall the event without the event recalling you. That is the change most people are actually after, and it is what EMDR is built to work toward.

Questions

Common questions about EMDR

Do you offer EMDR therapy in Winter Park?

Yes. Cristina Ally, LMHC, offers EMDR in person at our Winter Park office near Orlando, and by secure telehealth for clients anywhere in Florida.

Is EMDR evidence-based?

Yes. The World Health Organization's 2013 guidelines list EMDR among the therapies recommended for adults with PTSD, and the 2023 VA/DoD PTSD guideline gives it a strong recommendation.

Do I have to talk through every detail of what happened?

No. EMDR works with the memory itself, so you don't have to retell your story over and over to be free of it. You stay in charge of the pace the whole way.

Do you take insurance?

Sessions are self-pay: $185 for 50 minutes. If you have out-of-network benefits, we can give you a superbill to submit to your insurer for possible reimbursement, and HSA and FSA cards are welcome.

How do I get started?

Begin with a short phone consultation, about 10 minutes, so you can ask questions and get a feel for whether this is the right fit. You can request one anytime through the client portal.
