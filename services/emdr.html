Services/EMDR

EMDR

EMDR, for memories that never settled.

A gentle, structured way to help painful memories finally feel like the past, in Winter Park and Orlando, and by secure telehealth anywhere in Florida.

Request an appointment

Some memories don't settle. Years go by, life moves on, and a sound or a smell or an offhand comment still drops you right back into a moment you'd rather not visit. If that sounds familiar, I'm glad you found your way here. This is the kind of thing EMDR can help with.

Who EMDR is for

I use EMDR with adults, especially women and trauma survivors whose memories still feel raw. A lot of people come to EMDR after years of talking. They know their history, and they can explain exactly why they react the way they do. They still react that way. When there's a gap between what you know and what you feel, it often means a memory hasn't finished being processed, and that gap is what EMDR works on.

It might be worth a conversation if any of this sounds like you:

  • Your feelings sometimes seem bigger than the moment in front of you
  • Certain triggers set you off in a way you can't talk yourself out of
  • There's something you believe about yourself that you know isn't true

What EMDR is

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR) is a structured therapy that has been extensively researched and is widely used in the treatment of trauma. It follows a set of standardized steps, so you can know ahead of time roughly what a session will involve. For a lot of people, that predictability is what makes it feel safe enough to try.

How EMDR works

EMDR uses bilateral stimulation, which means gently activating both sides of the brain in turn, often with guided eye movements. Those eye movements resemble REM sleep, when the mind is thought to sort through the day, and EMDR appears to help the brain reprocess stuck memories in a similar way.

The memory doesn't disappear. What I hope for with you is that it finally feels like the past.

EMDR can also help with the beliefs that grew out of relational trauma, or out of abuse and neglect in childhood: that you're not safe, that it was somehow your fault, that you're too much or not enough. If you'd like a fuller explanation of the approach, the EMDR Institute has one.

What EMDR can help with

EMDR was first established as a treatment for PTSD. Since then it's been used in work with panic attacks, phobias, performance anxiety, disturbing memories, complicated grief, stress reduction, the effects of sexual or physical abuse, addictions, pain disorders, dissociative disorders, body dysmorphic disorders, and personality disorders.

You don't have to see yourself on that list for EMDR to be worth asking about.

How we'll work together

We don't start EMDR on day one. First comes safety and stabilization: building trust, and making sure you have grounding skills in place before we do any memory work. When we get there, protocol sessions are scheduled as extended sessions so we have room to finish. You set the pace, and nothing happens before you're ready.

How long it takes depends on you, your goals, your commitment, and what brought you in. I'll never keep you longer than is helpful, and you'll always know what the plan is.

EMDR is one part of my broader approach to trauma recovery, which also makes room for the life that comes after a memory is resolved.

Questions

Common questions about EMDR

Do you offer EMDR therapy in Winter Park?

Yes. I'm Cristina Ally, LMHC, and I offer EMDR in person at our Winter Park office near Orlando and by secure telehealth for clients anywhere in Florida.

Is EMDR evidence-based?

Yes. The World Health Organization's 2013 guidelines list EMDR among the therapies recommended for adults with PTSD, and the 2023 VA/DoD PTSD guideline gives it a strong recommendation.

Do I have to talk through every detail of what happened?

No. EMDR works with the memory itself, so you don't have to tell me every detail of what happened. You stay in charge of the pace the whole way.

Do you take insurance?

No, all sessions are self-pay. A session is $185 for 50 minutes, and EMDR is often scheduled as a double session. If you have out-of-network benefits, I can give you a superbill on request to send to your insurer, and HSA and FSA cards work too.

How do I get started?

Start with a 10-minute phone consultation. You can ask me anything about EMDR, and we'll see together whether it's the right fit. You can request one anytime through the client portal.
