Grief Counseling

Grief does not keep a schedule.

Grief and bereavement counseling for adults in Winter Park and Orlando, and by secure telehealth anywhere in Florida.

  • Who it's for: Adults moving through loss and grief, with a special focus on women
  • Where: In person at our Winter Park office on Aloma Avenue, near Orlando, or by secure telehealth anywhere in Florida
  • With: Cristina Ally, LMHC, NCC, trained in EMDR and Accelerated Resolution Therapy (ART)

Grief does not keep a schedule, and it does not care what anyone thinks the timeline should be.

Bereavement is the process of recovering from the death of someone you love. Grief is the reaction to loss of any kind, and it reaches well past death: the end of a marriage, a diagnosis, a miscarriage, a job, a friendship, a version of the future you had already started living in.

Both hold a wide range of feeling. Fear. Anger. Sadness that goes down further than you expected it to.

Every grieving experience is different

How a person adapts to loss changes dramatically from one person to the next, shaped by their background, their beliefs, their relationship with the person who died, and a dozen other things.

It also changes within the same person. Someone may be able to hold their daily routine together after one loss and be unable to get out of bed after another. That is not inconsistency and it is not weakness. It is what grief is.

What grief can look like

Grief symptoms are commonly physical, emotional, and social at once.

Physical

Crying and sighing. Headaches. Loss of appetite. Difficulty sleeping. Weakness. Fatigue that rest does not touch.

Emotional

Sadness and yearning. Worry. Anger. Frustration. Guilt, which is often the one people are least willing to say out loud.

Social

Feeling detached from people you are standing right next to. Pulling away from contact. Behaving in ways that are not like you.

If those are showing up in your life in ways that distress you, or that are not easing with time, that is worth taking seriously rather than waiting out.

Is it grief, or is it depression?

This is one of the most common questions people bring to a first session, and it is a good question. Grief and depression share a great deal of territory and they are not the same thing, and the difference matters because it changes what helps. Part of early grief work is sorting out which one you are in, and whether you are in both.

Grief that nobody else is treating as a loss

Not every loss comes with a funeral. A miscarriage. An estrangement. A divorce. A diagnosis that ends one version of your life. Losing someone who is still alive, to addiction or illness or a choice they made. The people around you may not know to offer you anything at all, which leaves you grieving and explaining at the same time.

Those losses are real, and they are workable.

How we work with grief

There is no protocol that makes a loss stop mattering, and the goal is not to get over it. The goal is to find a way to carry it that leaves room for the rest of your life.

In practice, that work often moves through telling the story of what happened, at whatever pace you can stand. Self-care, which in grief is less about indulgence than about basic maintenance when nothing feels worth doing. Relationships, which loss reorganizes whether you asked it to or not. The special events, the anniversaries, the birthdays, the empty chair at the holiday table, each of which arrives with its own weight. And eventually a new normal that you did not choose and still get to shape.

When words are not the easiest route in, sand tray is available inside individual sessions, which some people find useful for a loss that is larger than any sentence available for it.

When a death was sudden, violent, or witnessed, grief and trauma are braided together, and the trauma piece may need its own work. In those cases grief counseling runs alongside trauma therapy, including EMDR, which is used with complicated grief.

The dates that arrive whether you are ready or not

Grief keeps a calendar. The first birthday. The anniversary. The holiday table with a chair nobody wants to move. And then a random Tuesday in a grocery store aisle that turns out to be worse than either.

Part of this work is seeing those coming, so they are not ambushes, and deciding in advance what you want them to look like. Some people want the tradition kept exactly as it was. Some want it changed completely. Both are allowed, and neither one is a betrayal.

Grief in a group

Some people do this work better alongside others who are in it. Flourish has run grief support groups offering a safe, therapeutic environment to move through loss with other people who understand what that is like. Groups form periodically rather than running continuously. You can register your interest for the next one.

When it is time to reach out

There is no threshold you have to cross first. If grief-related thoughts, feelings, or behaviors are distressing you, that is reason enough.

Some things are worth reaching out about sooner rather than later: weeks of not sleeping or not eating, pulling away from everyone, guilt that has become the loudest thing in your head, a death that was sudden or violent, or a grief that simply is not moving and that you have run out of people to talk to about.

You do not have to be in crisis to deserve support, and you do not have to be far enough along for this to be the right time.

Questions

Common questions about grief counseling

Is what I'm feeling normal?

Grief doesn't follow a tidy timeline, and it can look like exhaustion, numbness, anger, or waves that arrive out of nowhere. If you're wondering whether it's grief or something more, this article may help, and so can talking it through.

Do I have to be ready to talk about the loss?

No. We start where you are. Some sessions are about the person or the loss itself, and some are simply about getting through the week.

Can grief counseling happen online?

Yes. Grief counseling is available in person in Winter Park or by secure telehealth anywhere in Florida.

Do you take insurance?

Sessions are self-pay: $185 for 50 minutes. If you have out-of-network benefits, we can give you a superbill to submit to your insurer for possible reimbursement, and HSA and FSA cards are welcome.

How do I get started?

Begin with a short phone consultation, about 10 minutes, so you can ask questions and get a feel for whether this is the right fit. You can request one anytime through the client portal.
