Trauma Recovery

Trauma recovery that does not require reliving it.

Trauma therapy for adults in Winter Park and Orlando, and by secure telehealth anywhere in Florida.

Most people experience trauma at some point. A car accident. Abuse or neglect. The sudden death of someone you love. A violent act, exposure to the violence of war, a natural disaster. Many people recover over time, carried by the support of the people around them, and find their own resilience on the other side of it.

Others discover that the event did not stay in the past. Long after it is over, they are still living with deep emotional pain, fear, confusion, or post-traumatic stress. If that is where you are, the support and guidance of a therapist is not a luxury. It is how the healing actually gets done.

When trauma does not fade on its own

There is no schedule that trauma is supposed to follow, and no threshold an event has to clear before it counts. What matters is what you are living with now.

The DSM-5 groups post-traumatic symptoms into four kinds, and most people recognize themselves in at least one of them.

Avoidance

Steering around specific places, sights, situations, and sounds because they bring the event back, along with the anxiety, depression, numbness, or guilt that arrives with them.

Re-experiencing

Intrusive thoughts. Nightmares. Flashbacks that put you back inside a moment you thought you had left.

Hyperarousal

Anger, irritability, and a kind of constant scanning that never fully switches off. Reckless or self-destructive behavior. Sleep that will not come or will not hold.

Negative mood and thinking

Losing interest in things you used to enjoy. Gaps and blurred edges where the details of the event should be. Changes in your habits or your behavior that started after the trauma and never changed back.

If you are reading that list and recognizing yourself, that recognition is useful information rather than a diagnosis. Naming what is happening is often the first thing that makes it feel workable.

Healing from trauma does not require reliving it

This is the fear that keeps most people out of a therapy room, and it is worth answering directly. You do not have to retell your story endlessly in order to be free of it.

The work tends to unfold in three movements.

Safety first

Before anything else, we build trust and give you tools to stay grounded. You set the pace. Nothing happens before you are ready for it, and "not yet" is always an acceptable answer.

Resolve, do not relive

When you are ready, we work with the memory itself, using structured trauma-resolution tools rather than open-ended retelling. This is also where grief lives, for the time that was taken and for the person you might have been without it.

Grow forward

With the past quieter, the work turns forward: boundaries, relationships, and a sense of yourself that is not organized around what happened.

The tools we use

Cristina Ally, LMHC is trained in Accelerated Resolution Therapy (ART), EMDR, and the Reconsolidation of Traumatic Memories.

EMDR

Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing uses bilateral stimulation to help the brain reprocess memories that got stuck, so they can be filed as things that happened rather than things that are still happening. Read more about EMDR.

ART

Accelerated Resolution Therapy is a structured protocol, usually delivered in an extended session, designed for working through a specific traumatic memory.

RTM

The Reconsolidation of Traumatic Memories is a third structured protocol, aimed at the same goal by a different route.

Which one fits depends on you, on what you are carrying, and on what you want out of the work. We decide that together, and protocol work does not begin until the groundwork is in place.

The work around the work

Trauma resolution is one part of trauma recovery, and on its own it is rarely the whole of it. Alongside the protocols, trauma therapy at Flourish makes room for what trauma tends to damage on its way through a life: attachment and the ability to let people close, boundaries, what a healthy relationship is supposed to feel like, patterns of revictimization, codependency, self-esteem, and the family-of-origin material underneath all of it.

That is slower work than a protocol session, and it is often where clients say the real change showed up.

Where language is not the easiest route in, sand tray is available inside individual sessions as another way to work.

Adult survivors of childhood abuse

A great deal of this work is with adults carrying something that started long before they had words for it. The event may be decades old. The conclusions drawn from it, about safety, about trust, about whether it was somehow your fault, are often still running the present. Those conclusions are workable, and they are frequently where trauma therapy does its most useful work.

You set the pace, from the first session to the last. Nothing in this work happens before you are ready for it.
