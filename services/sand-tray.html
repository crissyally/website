Services/Sand Tray

Sand Tray

Sand tray, for what words keep missing.

An expressive, hands-on modality for adults, offered in person at the Winter Park office and serving the greater Orlando area.

Some things resist being explained. You can describe the situation accurately, in order, with all the right words, and still walk out of the room carrying exactly what you walked in with. Sand tray is a way around that.

What sand tray is

Sand tray is a gentle, hands-on form of expressive therapy. There is a shallow tray of sand and a shelf of small objects: figures, animals, buildings, bridges, trees, ordinary things and strange ones. You choose what goes in the tray and where it goes. That is the whole instruction.

What you build becomes something to look at together, rather than something to account for. Nothing is graded, nothing has to be pretty, and there is no correct arrangement. The therapist stays alongside you rather than interpreting at you, and you keep authorship of what your own scene means.

No experience with art or craft is required. If you can put an object down in sand, you can do this.

An adult modality

At Flourish, sand tray is offered as an adult modality, used inside individual counseling with adult clients. It is not a lighter version of talking and it is not a novelty. It is a different route to the same material.

The reason it works so well with adults is fairly plain. Under stress, most of us get more articulate rather than less, and being articulate can quietly become its own form of distance. Building a scene sidesteps the explanation and goes at the thing directly.

Who tends to find it useful

  • People who have told their story many times, clearly and calmly, and have never once felt it move
  • People who go analytical under pressure, and who notice they are narrating rather than feeling
  • People carrying something from long before they had words for it
  • People sitting with a decision or a relationship that will not resolve on the page
  • People in grief, where the loss is larger than any sentence available for it
  • Anyone who would simply rather build something than describe it

It is also fine to be curious and skeptical at the same time. Most people arrive that way.

What a session looks like

A sand tray session is a normal individual session that happens to include the tray. We talk first, the same way we would otherwise. When it seems useful, you build. Some people work in silence for a long stretch. Some narrate the whole way through. Both are fine.

Afterward we look at what is there and talk about it, and that conversation tends to go somewhere different from the one we would have had without it. Nothing you build is kept, photographed, or shown to anyone, and the tray is yours to leave as it is or to change.

You are never required to use the tray. It is one tool available in the room, offered when it fits, and declined without explanation any time it does not.

Sand tray inside trauma work

Trauma frequently damages the route between an experience and the language for it, which is part of why retelling a story so often fails to shift it. Expressive work gives the material a different door.

Sand tray is used alongside the structured protocols in trauma recovery, including EMDR, rather than in place of them. It is also used in ordinary individual therapy where nothing traumatic is in view at all.

Expressive arts more broadly

Sand tray belongs to a wider family of expressive work: drawing, painting, image, and metaphor. Cristina is drawn to this approach because creativity reaches parts of experience that words have a hard time getting to. The making comes first, and the conversation that follows deepens what the work has already said.

Start when you are ready

If you have been explaining yourself perfectly and getting nowhere, this may be a different way in. You can request an appointment through the client portal, and mention that you are interested in sand tray.
